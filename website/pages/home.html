Video editor for Linux

The Resolve layout and keys, free and light on Linux.

Kinoklip is a free, open source video editor with the pages, menus and shortcuts of DaVinci Resolve. It is light enough for a laptop with integrated graphics, and H.264, H.265 and AAC are included.

The page bar

Seven pages, one job each

The buttons along the bottom of the window are the same as in Resolve. Press Shift+2 for Media up to Shift+8 for Deliver; Shift+4 is Edit.

Media page

Bring everything in

Look through your files and bring them into the project. The media pool keeps clips in bins, smart bins that fill themselves with the clips matching your rules, and power bins. Import a whole folder and its sub-folders become bins.

Getting started

{{> ill-media}}

Cut page

A quick rough cut

A simple editor for fast first versions. Two timelines sit one above the other: the whole edit, and a close-up of the part you are working on. The viewer can show one source clip, all your source clips as one continuous tape, or the timeline.

Getting started

{{> ill-cut}}

Edit page

The main editor

Tracks, trims, blade, transitions, titles and keyframes, with the keys you know: I and O mark, J K L play, B cuts. Most Resolve tutorials for the Edit page work here too.

Transitions · Keyframes

{{> ill-edit}}

Compositing page

Nodes, when layers are not enough

Build the picture of one clip from nodes: small boxes that each do one thing, such as show the clip, write a text, blur, key out green or combine two pictures, joined by lines. Merge and Transform in full, masks, Luma and Delta keyers, Motion Blur, and Spline and Keyframes panels.

Put text over a clip

{{> ill-comp}}

Color page

Grade every clip

Fix a shot that came out too dark or too blue, or give it a look. Lift, Gamma, Gain and Offset wheels, curves, a qualifier, power windows and LUTs, in a node graph per clip, plus a grade for the whole timeline. What you see here plays on the Edit page and is part of the exported video.

Colour correct a clip

{{> ill-color}}

Audio page

Mix and record

Mix the sound with track levels, meters, an equaliser and dynamics. Record a voice-over straight onto the timeline from any microphone your desktop sees, through PipeWire or PulseAudio.

Record a voice-over

Deliver page

Export for anywhere

Export the finished video to a file with a preset such as YouTube 1080p, in H.264 or H.265 with AAC sound. Jobs wait in a render queue, and Individual clips renders each clip to its own file, with handles and naming options.

Why on Linux

What Kinoklip does differently

Resolve runs on Linux, but its support is minimal: a short list of officially supported distributions, NVIDIA-first GPU support, and no H.264 or H.265 in the free version.

Kinoklip
Light on modest hardwareVA-API decoding on Intel and AMD, compositing on the GPU with plain OpenGL, the next clip prepared before a cut. Keyframes, titles and animations play without lag on integrated graphics.
CodecsH.264, H.265 and AAC for import and export, for free. Resolve's free version on Linux has none of them, and Studio adds H.264 and H.265 only with an NVIDIA GPU.
RecordingPipeWire and PulseAudio on Wayland and X11. Resolve on Linux talks only to ALSA, so on most desktops recording a microphone needs extra setup.
GraphicsAny GPU with OpenGL 3.3 AMD, Intel and NVIDIA, integrated graphics too, which Resolve doesn't officially support.
LayoutLike Resolve The same pages and shortcuts.
LicenceGPL-3.0 Free and open source, shipped as one AppImage.

On Windows or macOS: don't. Kinoklip isn't available there yet, and even when it is, Resolve is the more complete and more mature editor. It's a great project, built and maintained by people who are paid to work on it full time, and that's worth supporting. Its free version is excellent, and buying Studio supports the people who made the workflow Kinoklip copies.
