# Clean-room policy

Kinoklip is an independent implementation of a non-linear video editor whose layout, behaviour and
default shortcuts follow DaVinci Resolve's. These rules keep the project legally independent of
Blackmagic Design. They apply to everyone who changes the repository, people and AI agents alike.
Breaking them is the most serious mistake a contributor can make here.

## What we use
- **General knowledge of how Resolve behaves**: which panels exist and where they sit, what a
  command does, which key triggers it. Behaviour, layout ideas and keyboard shortcut assignments
  are functional facts, not artwork; we re-create them in our own code, words and pictures.
- **Public, open specifications and libraries**: Qt, KDE Frameworks, FFmpeg, OpenGL, the
  freedesktop.org standards, file-format specifications, papers.
- **Feedback from the project owner** in words and screenshots of Kinoklip itself.
- **Other open-source editors.** When we're stuck on how to do something, read how Kdenlive,
  Shotcut, Olive, OpenShot, Blender's video editor, Natron, Ardour, Audacity or MLT solve it.
  Reading their code for ideas is always fine. Copying code is fine only when its licence is
  compatible with GPL-3.0-or-later (GPL-2.0-or-later, GPL-3.0, LGPL, MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0; not
  GPL-2.0-only), and only with its copyright notice and a line in
  [attribution.md](../attribution.md), see rule 6 below.

## What we never do
1. **Never copy anything from Resolve or other Blackmagic products**: no code, shaders, icons,
   cursors, images, splash screens, UI artwork, fonts, LUTs, presets, templates, sample media,
   project files, configuration or keymap files, and no text from the manuals, help or website.
   No traced, redrawn or recoloured copies of their icons: our icons follow generic conventions (a
   play triangle, a razor blade) and are drawn from scratch.
2. **Never install, run, disassemble, decompile or inspect Resolve** (or its bundles, libraries,
   databases or file formats) while developing Kinoklip. The AI agents that write Kinoklip work
   without any access to Resolve. (Resolve was once installed on the development machine before
   this rule; it was removed on the owner's request without being inspected, see status.md.)
3. **Never commit screenshots of Resolve** or other proprietary software. Screenshots in the
   repository show only Kinoklip, with media we generated ourselves or media that is openly
   licensed.
4. **Never use Blackmagic names for our own features**: generic words (Cut, Edit, Color, Deliver,
   Media, Audio, Compositing) are fine; "Fusion", "Fairlight" and other product names are not.
   "DaVinci Resolve" appears only to describe compatibility (for example the name of the keymap
   profile that mimics its shortcuts) and in the trademark notice.
5. **Never suggest affiliation or endorsement**: no Blackmagic logos, no wording like "official"
   or "by the makers of".
6. **Never copy GPL-incompatible or unlicensed code** from anywhere. Code from other open-source
   projects comes in only with its licence, its copyright notice and a line in
   [attribution.md](../attribution.md).

## Assets
Every non-code asset (image, icon, font, colour scheme, sound, sample media, preset) must be one of:
- original work made for Kinoklip (by the owner or generated by the project's own tools), or
- openly licensed (GPL-compatible) with its licence and author recorded.

Every asset gets a row in [attribution.md](../attribution.md) in the same change. When in doubt,
leave it out and make our own.

## Honesty about how Kinoklip is made
A traditional clean room uses two separate teams: one studies the original, the other writes code
from a written description without ever seeing it. Kinoklip is built by one owner directing AI
models. The models were trained on large amounts of public code and text, so we cannot claim the
two-team separation. What we can and do guarantee is the list above: no Blackmagic material is
read, used or copied while Kinoklip is made, and everything shipped is original or openly licensed.
